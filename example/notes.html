What the steel does after eleven years.

A short piece on patina, oil, and why we stopped lacquering anything.

Lacquer solves the first year and ruins the next twenty. It yellows, it chips at the corners, and once it fails it traps moisture underneath exactly where the metal is thinnest. We stopped using it in 2016 and the warranty claims fell by roughly half.

Wearing in

Bare steel takes on a grey cast where the hand sits. Some people call this damage. It is the tool recording where you hold it, and it does not affect tolerance.

  • Steel: wipe with a dry cloth, oil twice a year.
  • Walnut: the same oil, less of it, never soak.
  • Iron: keep it out of the sink. That is the whole guide.

If a piece ever fails — rusted through, chipped, out of true — send it back. We replace it and keep the old one on the wall.

Care, in full

Two minutes, twice a year:

wipe  — dry cloth, along the grain
oil   — three drops, camellia or mineral
wait  — ten minutes, then wipe the excess
